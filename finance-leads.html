Questions

What finance leads ask us, answered plainly.

Is it a real general ledger?

Yes. Double entry, cash or accrual, US GAAP or IFRS, several entities and currencies, with locked periods. It is the system of record, not a layer on top of one.

How do I control what the agents do?

A policy you write sets what agents may post alone and what waits for a person. Nothing posts outside those limits.

Can I check the work?

Each entry shows its document, its match and its reasoning. You can reclassify or post a manual journal at any time, and it is logged.

How fast is the close, honestly?

Days, not weeks, and it depends on you. Agents reconcile entries as they arrive, so what is left at month end is the exceptions, the judgment calls and your sign-off. The more your policy lets agents post alone, the less is left. A bill that arrives on the 4th still arrives on the 4th, so we do not promise a close on day zero.

I am a fractional CFO with several clients. Does it fit?

Yes. The books are current when you arrive, so the first hour is not spent fixing them. You get one sign-in across clients, and the forecast, scenarios and board pack for each run on that client's own ledger. Your access to each client is set by that client and can carry an end date.

Does it handle several entities and currencies?

Yes, that is in the design from the start. Each company keeps its own books in its own currency, and the group view consolidates them and removes what they charge each other.

What about audit?

Every entry carries its document, its approver and its history, and the schedules an auditor asks for are prepared as you go. It prepares you for an audit. It does not replace one.
