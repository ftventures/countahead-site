Groups

Several companies, one set of numbers.

Each company keeps its own books, in its own currency and under its own country's rules. The group view adds them up, removes what they charge each other and translates the rest, every day and not only at year end.

Accounting rules

Cash or accrual, official or adjusted, one set of entries.

Different readers need different views of the same business. They all come from one ledger, with the difference between them shown line by line.

Approvals

Two hundred entries, checked in two minutes.

Finance teams already approve in batches. What makes it safe is everything around the click: rules you wrote, a sample you check, exceptions kept out, and a record of who approved what.

Controls

Every action has an owner, a limit and a record.

Automation is only useful in finance if you can see exactly what it did and why. So here is who may do what, which months are closed for good, and the full history of any entry.

Policies

Write the rule once. The software follows it.

Two rulebooks, kept apart. One tells the agents what they may do alone, when they must ask and what they may send. The other is the company handbook for your people. Each policy is a few numbers. Change one and the written rules rewrite themselves, the software follows them, and everyone is asked to read the new version.

Tax

Tax-ready books, every day of the year.

Sales tax and VAT by place, the adjustments your income tax return needs, contractor forms and every deadline, kept current as the books are written. Your adviser files; nothing is a scramble.

Audit readiness

Run your own audit before the auditors do.

Over a thousand checks run every night. Sales are sample-tested against contracts, the auditor's usual requests are answered in advance, and any number traces to the money in the bank.
