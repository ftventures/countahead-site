About

Finance has two halves. They have never shared a ledger.

Accounting records what happened. Planning decides what happens next. Countahead is being built so both read the same numbers.

Why it exists

The books are mandatory. The future is where the value is.

Every company has to keep books. The law asks for them, and so do investors and the bank. They are mostly a record of things that have already happened.

The questions that decide where a company ends up point the other way. Can we afford this hire? When does the cash run out? What happens if our biggest customer leaves?

Today those questions get answered in a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet is fed by an export from the accounting system, which is fed by the billing tool, which someone keeps in step with the customer list by hand. Every copy is a chance for the numbers to disagree.

Countahead puts the whole chain on one record, so the plan is as current as the books and nobody has to copy anything.

Why now

Why this can be built now.

Three things are true today that were not a few years ago.

01AI can do the routine finance work

Reading invoices, matching the bank, drafting contracts, chasing late payers, preparing the month-end close. Agents do the first pass and a person checks what matters.

02One system can cover the whole company

Customers, contracts, billing, books, planning and company records used to mean six tools from six vendors. Building all of it on one ledger is now realistic.

03So the plan can run on real numbers

With everything on one record, the forecast, the board pack and the data room are always current. Change one thing and see what it does to cash.

Our principles

Six things you can count on.

01Accounting first

If the books are not solid, nothing built on them is worth reading. The ledger is real double entry, with period locks and a full audit trail.

Questions

Asked often, answered plainly.

Does Countahead replace my accountant?

No. It changes what their time goes on. Agents take the entry work, inside rules you or your accountant set, and the judgement, review and advice stay with a person. Filings and tax advice stay with a qualified accountant or tax adviser, who gets the books and the evidence ready for them.

Who decides what goes into my books?

You do. A policy you write sets what agents may post alone, for example small routine bills from a supplier you already know. Everything else waits for a person, routine entries can be approved together, and every action is logged.

Is it only for software companies?

No. It is for startups first, in any sector: software, services, online stores and others. The analytics and board packs change with the kind of business.

Where will it be available?

The United States first. Several entities and currencies are part of the design from the start, so companies that work across borders are in scope, and more countries follow.

Will it work with the tools I already use?

That is the plan. Banks, payment processors, payroll, online stores and the main sales tools are on the list. None is live yet. See the planned integrations.

Is it certified or independently audited?

Not yet. We will not show a badge or name a standard until it has been earned.

What will it cost?

Pricing is not set.

Can I use it today?

Not yet. Countahead is in development. Leave your email and we will write when early access opens.
